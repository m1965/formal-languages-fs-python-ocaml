\chapter{LR conflicts and precedence with Larkita}
\section{Shift-reduce and reduce-reduce conflicts}
An ambiguous arithmetic grammar has several viable actions after a recognized expression.
The Larkita grammar below deliberately retains the ambiguous grammar.  Larkita builds the canonical LR(1) automaton, resolves every shift/reduce conflict in favour of shifting, and reports it with a warning; a reduce/reduce conflict is an error.
@@snippet:lalr-ambiguous@@

Larkita writes all states with their lookahead tokens and all conflicts into an output file; state numbers from another tool are not evidence about these states.
\href{../../Chapter-10/01-Conflicts.ipynb}{\nolinkurl{Chapter-10/01-Conflicts.ipynb}} shows the actual conflict diagnostics.
Larkita has no precedence declarations.  Instead, the grammar is stratified with one syntactical variable per precedence level.

Left-recursive rules make an operator associate to the left, right-recursive rules make it associate to the right, and a variable closer to the start symbol has lower precedence.
Thus subtraction parses as $(1-2)-3$, multiplication binds above addition, and exponentiation parses as $2^{(3^4)}$.
